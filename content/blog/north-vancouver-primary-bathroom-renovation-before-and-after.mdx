In a bathroom, the details behind the tile matter just as much as the ones you see. This North Vancouver primary bathroom looked tired — an older double vanity, builder-basic lighting, a dated shower and tub — but the real opportunity was to rebuild it properly: waterproofing, heated floors, a larger shower, and finishes that finally felt calm.

Here's how BRIO took it from dated and inefficient to a comfortable, everyday-functional primary suite — with the before-and-after and a look at the work behind the walls.

## The Homeowners' Challenge

The existing primary bathroom no longer matched how the homeowners wanted to start and end their day. It had storage and a double-sink layout, but the lighting was harsh, the shower didn't feel as generous as it could, and the finishes made the room feel more functional than relaxing.

For a home with older character, the goal was to update the bathroom without making it feel disconnected from the rest of the house — cleaner and calmer, while still supporting real daily use.

## The Plan: What We Set Out to Change

The plan focused on a more comfortable, durable, and visually quiet bathroom while improving how the room worked day to day.

<CalloutCard tone="key" title="Project goals">

- Improve functionality and everyday comfort
- A cleaner, brighter, calmer look
- More practical storage
- Better lighting and ventilation
- Modernized fixtures and finishes
- Handle the behind-the-wall work properly

The design direction balanced warm wood tones with soft stone-look surfaces and clean fixtures. A larger double shower became the key functional upgrade, while the vanity, lighting, tile, and heated floors shifted the room from dated and busy to calm and cohesive.

## Inside the Build

This phase is why bathroom renovations depend on careful sequencing. Demolition reveals what's happening behind the walls, then plumbing, electrical, framing, cement board, waterproofing, heated-floor prep, tile, glass, vanity, lighting, and fixtures all have to be coordinated in the right order. The finished room looks simple and calm — that calm depends on a disciplined build.

## The Final Reveal

The finished bathroom feels dramatically different — a softer visual rhythm, better lighting, a more refined vanity, and a larger shower that feels purposeful rather than squeezed into the old layout.

<Figure src="/images/blog/north-vancouver-primary-bathroom-renovation-before-and-after/08-final-shower-and-vanity.webp" alt="Finished North Vancouver bathroom with double shower, vanity and glass enclosure" caption="The larger shower and updated vanity now work together as one cohesive primary suite." width={1080} height={810} />

The updated finishes give the bathroom a calmer tone, while the functional changes make it easier to use every day — the kind of renovation that changes both the look of a room and how you experience your morning and evening routines.

## Builder's Notes

At BRIO, we see bathroom renovations as technical projects as much as design projects. Beautiful tile, mirrors, lighting, and fixtures matter — but the long-term success of a bathroom depends on the work that happens before those finishes are installed.

<PullQuote attribution="BRIO Construction">A good bathroom should look polished on day one and keep performing for years — which comes down to waterproofing, sequencing, and decisions made with the whole room in mind.</PullQuote>

When those decisions are handled properly, the bathroom becomes more than an updated finish package — it becomes a comfortable, reliable part of the home.
